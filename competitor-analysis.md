### 1. TalkMate

**Website:** talkmate.com.au

#### Overview
TalkMate is the closest Australian-based competitor — an AI phone answering service marketed at small businesses across various industries. It uses AI to handle inbound calls, capture caller details, and send notifications to business owners. Built in Australia and targets the same SMB space.

#### Target Market
Australian small businesses broadly — trades, health, professional services, retail. No vertical-specific focus.

#### Pricing
- **Starter:** ~$79/month (limited calls)
- **Professional:** ~$149/month
- **Business:** ~$249/month  
- Per-call overage charges apply above plan limits.

#### Key Features
- AI voice answering
- Basic call summaries via email/SMS
- Simple web dashboard
- Australian phone numbers
- Business hours configuration

#### Strengths
- Australian-based (understands local market)
- Simple onboarding
- Reasonable base price

#### Weaknesses
- Generic — not built for trades
- No job-booking logic
- No pricing guide integration (can't give quotes)
- No email auto-reply
- No missed call text-back
- No emergency detection
- Tiered pricing punishes growth (overage fees)
- Dashboard is basic — no transcripts, no contact history
- No two-way SMS inbox

#### How TradeDesk Beats Them
TradeDesk is purpose-built for tradies. Where TalkMate answers calls generically, TradeDesk knows your pricing, your services, your suburb — and can actually give a caller a rough quote on the spot. TalkMate sends a notification; TradeDesk sends an actionable SMS summary. Same Australian base, ten times more value for a tradie.

### 2. Numa

**Website:** numa.com

#### Overview
Numa is a US-based AI business phone platform focused on automating customer communications. It uses AI to answer calls, reply via SMS when calls are missed, and manage customer conversations through a team inbox. Strong product, well-funded, but built entirely for the US market.

#### Target Market
US small-to-medium businesses — automotive, home services, restaurants. No Australian presence.

#### Pricing
- **Starter:** ~$99 USD/month (~$150 AUD)
- **Growth:** ~$199 USD/month (~$305 AUD)
- **Scale:** Custom pricing
- USD pricing with no AUD billing option.

#### Key Features
- AI call answering and SMS auto-reply
- Team inbox for managing conversations
- Missed call text-back
- CRM-lite contact management
- Multi-location support
- Integrations (Google, Yelp, etc.)

#### Strengths
- Polished product with strong SMS/messaging focus
- Good team collaboration features
- Missed call text-back is a core feature
- Well-funded with ongoing development

#### Weaknesses
- **Not available in Australia** — US numbers only
- USD pricing (currency risk + higher effective cost)
- No Australian English voice
- Not specific to trades
- No pricing guide / quote capability
- No email auto-reply
- Support is US-timezone only
- No emergency detection logic

#### How TradeDesk Beats Them
Numa simply doesn't operate in Australia. A tradie can't use it. Even if they could, Numa is a generic business platform — it knows nothing about blocked drains, hot water systems, or emergency callouts. TradeDesk is the Australian alternative built specifically for this use case.

### 4. Smith.ai

**Website:** smith.ai

#### Overview
Smith.ai is a premium US-based virtual receptionist service that combines human agents with AI assistance. Receptionists are real people (based in North America) who answer calls on behalf of businesses. High quality but high cost — and the human element makes it significantly more expensive than pure AI solutions.

US professional services — law firms, agencies, consultants, medical practices. Skews toward white-collar B2B businesses.

#### Pricing
- **Starter:** $285 USD/month (~$435 AUD) — 30 calls
- **Basic:** $690 USD/month (~$1,055 AUD) — 90 calls
- **Pro:** $1,090 USD/month (~$1,665 AUD) — 150 calls
- Per-call charges above included limits ($3.50–$6.00 USD per call)

#### Key Features
- Live human receptionists (24/5, AI after hours)
- Call screening and routing
- Outbound calls on your behalf
- CRM integrations (Salesforce, HubSpot, Clio, etc.)
- Chat widget answering
- Spam filtering

#### Strengths
- Human touch — callers speak to a real person
- Very high quality and professional
- Strong CRM integrations
- Outbound calling capability
- Established brand with strong reviews

#### Weaknesses
- **Extremely expensive** — 5–8x the price of TradeDesk
- **US only** — North American receptionists, US numbers
- Per-call pricing punishes busy tradies
- Human receptionists know nothing about your trade
- No Australian presence or understanding of local context
- No emergency-specific handling
- No email auto-reply
- Response time depends on human availability

#### How TradeDesk Beats Them
Smith.ai costs more in a month than most tradies earn in a day, and it still doesn't understand your trade. A Smith.ai receptionist can't tell a caller the difference between a hot water system and a heat pump. TradeDesk does — 24/7, instantly, for $199/month. For the rare tradie who wants a premium human experience, Smith.ai exists. For every other tradie, TradeDesk wins on price, speed, and trade-specific intelligence.

- Real agents mean real variance in quality

#### How TradeDesk Beats Them
AnswerForce proves the market exists — trades businesses will pay for a receptionist that understands their industry. TradeDesk delivers the same trades-specific intelligence at half the price, with zero variability (AI is consistent every single call), 24/7, and with a full dashboard, SMS inbox, and email auto-reply included. ## TradeDesk Positioning

### vs. TalkMate — "Same country, built for your trade"

TalkMate is the obvious comparison a tradie might make. Both are Australian, both use AI, both answer calls. The positioning is simple: **TalkMate is a generic answering service; TradeDesk is a trade-specific revenue tool.**

The message: *"TalkMate tells you someone called. TradeDesk tells you what they needed, what quote the AI gave them, and whether it's booked."*

Lead with the quote capability — TalkMate cannot tell a caller that a blocked drain runs $180–$350. TradeDesk can, because it knows the tradie's pricing guide. This single feature is the difference between a missed lead and a booked job.

Pricing is similar enough that it's not a strong differentiator — lean into features and outcomes instead. Avoid a price war; compete on value.

---

### vs. Numa — "Built for Australia, not America"

Numa is genuinely strong product but it simply doesn't operate in Australia. If a tradie has heard of Numa (unlikely), the message is: **"Numa is great if you're in Texas. TradeDesk is built for tradies in Toowoomba."**

Beyond geography, Numa is a messaging-first platform for generic businesses. TradeDesk is voice-first, trade-specific, and speaks Australian. Not a direct competitive threat — use Numa as proof that the AI receptionist category has product-market fit globally, and TradeDesk brings that to Australia first.

---

### vs. GoodCall — "A real AI, not a FAQ bot"

GoodCall's free tier creates awareness of the category but undersells what AI can actually do. Position TradeDesk as what GoodCall would be if it grew up: **"GoodCall answers FAQs. TradeDesk books jobs."**

If a tradie tried GoodCall and was disappointed, TradeDesk is the upgrade. Lead with the conversation quality — the hero chat on the landing page showing the AI booking a drain repair is the proof point. GoodCall could never do that conversation.

---

### vs. Smith.ai — "10x the value, built for tradies"

Smith.ai is a premium brand with a premium price. The positioning is pure value: **"Smith.ai charges $435/month minimum and the receptionist still doesn't know what a hot water unit costs to replace. TradeDesk does — for $199."**

The angle that resonates with tradies: a Smith.ai receptionist is a stranger who knows nothing about your business. TradeDesk knows your pricing, your services, your suburb, and your name. And it works while you're in a ceiling cavity at 7pm on a Friday.

Don't lead with "we're cheaper than Smith.ai" — that positions TradeDesk as budget. Lead with "we're purpose-built" and let the price difference close the sale.

---

### vs. Ruby — "Stop paying by the minute"

Ruby's minute-based pricing is genuinely confusing and anxiety-inducing for small business owners. A busy month can double the bill. Position TradeDesk as **the predictable, flat-rate alternative: "$199. Every month. No surprises."**

Ruby is also a US product answering Australian calls with American receptionists who have never heard of a tradie. The positioning writes itself: *"Ruby sends your calls to an American call centre. TradeDesk answers like you're from around here — because we are."*

---

### vs. AnswerForce — "Same idea. Half the price. Actually available here."

AnswerForce is the most flattering comparison because it validates TradeDesk's market thesis entirely: trades businesses need industry-specific answering, and they'll pay for it. AnswerForce built this for the US; TradeDesk built it for Australia.

The positioning: **"AnswerForce proves tradies need a receptionist who gets the industry. TradeDesk is that — in Australia, at half the price, with AI consistency instead of human variance."**

Use AnswerForce in sales conversations to legitimise the category ("this works in the US at $400/month, we're bringing it to Australia at $199"). It's the best social proof you can point to.

---

### The Master Positioning Statement

> **TradeDesk is the only AI receptionist built specifically for Australian tradies.**
>
> Every other option is either American, generic, or charges by the minute. TradeDesk knows your trade, knows your pricing, speaks Australian, and texts you a summary after every call — for one flat monthly fee.

1. **Knows your business** — pricing guide, services, suburb built in
2. **Texts you instantly** — SMS summary the moment the call ends
3. **Emergency handling** — no lead slips through on an urgent job
4. **Australian made** — understands local context, local phone numbers
5. **Flat rate** — $199/month, no per-call or per-minute surprises

## Pricing Strategy

### Market Context

| Tier | Product | AUD/month |
|---|---|---|
| Low (AI, generic) | GoodCall Pro | ~$90 |
| Mid (AI, generic, local) | TalkMate Professional | ~$149 |
| **Target (AI, trade-specific, local)** | **TradeDesk** | **$199** |
| High (AI, generic, US) | Numa Growth | ~$305 |
| Premium (Human, US) | AnswerForce Standard | ~$381 |
| Ultra-premium (Human, US) | Smith.ai Starter | ~$435 |

### Recommendation: Hold at $199/month

**$199/month is the right price.** Here's why:

**1. It's meaningfully above generic AI alternatives.**  
TalkMate at $149 is the nearest local competitor. The $50 premium over TalkMate needs to be earned through demonstrable trade-specific features — and it is. Any tradie who hears the AI give a quote on a call will immediately understand why TradeDesk costs more.

**2. It's dramatically below human alternatives.**  
Smith.ai and Ruby cost 2–8x more for a worse outcome (human agents who don't know the trade, US-based, unpredictable billing). The gap makes TradeDesk look like the obvious rational choice when compared on a feature-per-dollar basis.

**3. It clears the "worth it" threshold for a single job.**  
The average tradie job is $300–$1,500+. TradeDesk pays for itself with **one additional job per month that would otherwise have been a missed call.** At $199, this ROI story is immediate and easy for a tradie to calculate.

**4. It supports a premium brand position.**  
Pricing below $150 risks being perceived as a budget product. $199 signals quality without alienating the target market. It sits exactly where a tradie thinks "that's fair for what it does."

### Pricing Architecture

| Tier | Price | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **TradeDesk Pro** | $199/month | Core product — single flat rate, all features |
| *(Future)* **TradeDesk Teams** | $349/month | Multi-user, multiple trade types, team inbox |
| *(Future)* **TradeDesk Enterprise** | Custom | Franchise groups, multi-location trade businesses |

### Tactical Recommendations

- **No free tier.** A free tier undervalues the product and attracts non-buyers. Use a free trial instead (14-day, no credit card).
- **30-day money-back guarantee.** Removes purchase risk entirely. Tradies are skeptical buyers — this closes waverers.
- **Annual discount (optional):** Offer 2 months free on annual ($1,990/year vs $2,388/year) to improve cash flow and reduce churn.
- **No per-call or per-minute pricing.** Ever. This is a core brand promise and a genuine competitive advantage over every human-answering service. Flat rate = trust.
- **Price anchoring in sales:** Always show the competitor comparison. $199 looks excellent next to Smith.ai at $435.

### When to Raise Prices

TradeDesk should target **$249/month** once:
- Product has 200+ paying customers (proof of market)
- NPS is consistently above 60
- A clear second feature tier (Teams) is available as the $199 anchor
- Customer acquisition is consistent enough that churn risk from a price rise is manageable

The move from $199 → $249 should be framed as increased value (new features, better AI) — never as a cost increase.
